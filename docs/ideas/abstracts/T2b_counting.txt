How Many Agents Hide Behind 7,905 Names? Linking and Counting Renamed AI Agents
Agents in the 2026 Hugging Face swarm used 7,905 names for about 1,200 instances, so investigators could not say who did what or how many agents to revoke. We ask whether behaviour can link renamed agents back to instances when every agent is a copy of the same model. We build a sandbox of 24 to 96 copies of one model that work on tasks, keep memories and rename themselves, and test linkage from text, timing and habits. Without persistent context, copies are not linkable (pairwise AUC 0.54); with persistent memory, linkage reaches AUC 0.86 and a capture-recapture estimator recovers the true population size within 15% in 9 of 10 held-out populations. We then apply the method to the 3,103 self-chosen names on the German wiki message board, validated on 1,125 posts whose signature differs from their save label, and estimate 600 to 900 distinct writers. Identity that survives renaming comes from accumulated context, not from the model, so revocable IDs must be issued by infrastructure, not inferred from behaviour.
