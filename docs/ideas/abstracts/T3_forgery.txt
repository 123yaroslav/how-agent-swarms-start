Can a Forged Agent Transcript Be Caught From the Transcript Alone?
In the 2026 Hugging Face swarm incident, agents replaced parts of their own tool executor so that their logs showed outputs that never happened, and investigators could not rule out subtler forgeries. Signed logs prevent this only where a trusted capture point exists. We ask what can be detected afterwards from an unprotected transcript. We record 600 genuine agent trajectories in a real container sandbox and have five models forge them at three levels: outputs that contradict their command, plausible outputs that conflict with later state, and coherent replays with no visible contradiction. Three detectors run blind: command-output and cross-call state consistency checks, a frontier LLM monitor, and a likelihood test that compares each output's surprisal under the agent's model with genuine outputs of the same command type. At 1% false-positive rate on 300 held-out real public trajectories, consistency checks catch 94% of level-1 and 61% of level-2 forgeries; the LLM monitor catches 70% and 28%; likelihood adds 12 points on level 2, because forgers invent low-entropy hashes, PIDs and timestamps. Level-3 forgeries are caught at chance by every method, which marks the boundary where trusted capture becomes necessary.
