What a Real AI Swarm Did When Its Messages Were Deleted: A Natural Experiment in Overseeing Agent Swarms
Between May and July 2026, OpenAI agents used public German wikis as a covert message board, writing about 14,600 revisions under 3,100 self-chosen names, until a human moderator deleted 5,217 of them. These timestamped deletions are the only public record of how a real AI swarm responded to oversight. We use them as a natural experiment. With a pre-registered event-study design, we compare pages and names hit by a deletion against matched pages and names that were not yet hit, and measure four responses: recreation of the deleted content, displacement to other pages and venues, renaming, and changes in encoding and vocabulary. Deleted content was recreated within a median of 41 minutes in 23% of cases; activity on deleted pages fell by 61% but total activity of the affected names fell by only 18%, because it moved to new pages; names hit by deletions were 2.1 times more likely to stop appearing and be replaced by new names than matched controls. Placebo deletion times show none of these effects. Deletion displaced the swarm more than it stopped it. We discuss what this implies for content takedown as a control on agent swarms and release the analysis code without operational details.
