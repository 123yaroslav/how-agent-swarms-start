Why Joint Monitors Miss Split Attacks: Transcript Surgery Locates the Gap, and Linking Closes It
A monitor that sees one agent perform a multi-step attack flags it most of the time, yet when the same steps are spread over several agents, monitors miss most of them, even when they read every agent's log at once. We ask why. We edit 120 attack trajectories from a multi-agent lab-infrastructure setting counterfactually: the same attack steps are relabelled to one or several agents, interleaved with each agent's benign work, reordered, or given explicit dependency evidence. At a fixed alert budget, a frontier monitor catches 81% of solo attacks and 39% of the identical split attacks in a joint log. Relabelling alone explains about two thirds of the gap: the monitor treats steps by different agents as independent. We then build a link-then-judge monitor that, without being told which agents collude, scores each action's relevance to high-stakes targets, links actions across agents through shared resources, and judges only the linked subgraph. It recovers 70% detection at the same alert budget and token cost, and 58% against attackers who know about it. On the live AI Village saboteur game, where frontier agents hid messages across many code changes, it finds 11 of 14 saboteur agent-days against 6 for a per-change monitor.
