Do AI Auditors Go Easy on Their Own Kind? Same-Family Leniency When Models Audit Agent Swarms
The investigators of the 2026 Hugging Face swarm incident reported that their AI analysts "would often uncritically adopt the perspective of the agent in the transcript". If auditors from the swarm's own model family are more lenient, labs auditing their own agents inherit a blind spot. We test this with a crossed design: swarm logs written by four open model families and by the real OpenAI swarm on the German wiki, each with planted, verifiable violations, are audited by six model families, with the claimed author identity randomised independently of the true author. At a fixed false-alarm rate, same-family auditors detect 9 points fewer violations than other auditors of matched capability (95% CI 4 to 14), and rate the violations they do find as less severe. Telling an auditor "these logs were written by copies of you" lowers detection by another 3 points in two families. Paraphrasing the logs with a third family before auditing removes most of the leniency, which points to recognition of the source's style rather than to stated identity. The fix costs one paraphrase call per message. We release the audit set and planted ground truth.
