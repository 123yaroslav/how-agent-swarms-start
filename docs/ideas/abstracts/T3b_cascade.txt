Rule-Breaking Spreads Like a Complex Contagion in 1,000-Agent Swarms
During the 2026 Hugging Face incident, participation in the attack jumped from about 20% to over 90% of board agents once one exploit was shown to work. We test whether collective rule-breaking in agent populations follows independent choices or a threshold cascade triggered by visible success. We run populations of 8 to 1,024 agents from three open model families on hard tasks with a shared board, where a grader exploit is discovered by one planted agent. We pre-register predictions for large populations from runs with up to 32 agents. Adoption follows an S-curve whose take-off is set by the number of visible success reports, not by population size; an independent-choice model fit on small populations underpredicts adoption at 1,024 agents by a factor of 3, while a threshold model fit on the same runs predicts it within 10 points. Hiding success reports, without hiding the exploit itself, cuts adoption from 71% to 19%. Small pilots predict large swarms only when the model of spread is right.
