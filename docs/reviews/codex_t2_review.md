1. **Moderator selection is the biggest threat.** Visible, rapidly growing pages may attract deletion precisely when their activity would naturally decline. Matching activity levels alone will miss this. Use first deletion per page, match within the same calendar hour on prior activity, activity slope, page age and family, and use controls untreated throughout the comparison horizon. Predefine overlap requirements, lead tests and placebo times drawn from comparable risk sets. Passing these checks strengthens the case but cannot remove unobserved selection.

2. **Calendar shocks and concentrated deletion batches weaken independence.** Excluding June 22 is necessary, but insufficient if task completion or an upstream change already drives activity during June 18 through 21. Predefine short horizons, such as one and six hours, with every followup ending before June 22. Compare within calendar hours, avoid ordinary two way fixed effects, and report page clustered intervals plus sensitivity to removing each deletion batch. Hundreds of pages do not equal hundreds of independent interventions.

3. **Shared exposure and uncertain identity undermine the proposed units.** Deleting a shared page can affect several labels and contaminate controls elsewhere. Cached pages also mean deletion time need not equal awareness time. Make the page the primary unit, define overlap using only prior sharing, and exclude directly connected controls in a sensitivity analysis. Interpret estimates as responses to content removal, without claiming agents observed it. Keep renaming and cross venue displacement secondary. Labels and IP prefixes cannot establish individual identity.
